Overview

A free map of where people have actually seen wild animals in America's national parks. Research-grade iNaturalist observations and reviewed GBIF records for mammals and birds, gathered park by park, deduplicated, and drawn as hexagonal cells about 170 metres across. On top of that: species pages, a route planner over the park's real roads and trails, live USGS stream and earthquake data, and a 3D tour of each park's landmarks. No server, no accounts, no tracking, and nothing paid anywhere in the chain.

The question

Where in a park have people actually seen this animal?

Answerable from public records, if you are willing to do the reconciliation. Two occurrence databases with different review standards, overlapping records and no shared identifier are merged park by park, deduplicated, and binned into hexagons small enough to point at a trailhead. Eleven parks are live; an unattended pipeline is working through the remaining 52.

Why it matters

The data exists and is public, and it is close to unusable in its raw form: millions of point records across two APIs, one of which mirrors the other, with licences that differ per photograph. Turning that into something a person can open on a phone at a trailhead is entirely a data-engineering problem, and the honest version of it involves throwing a lot of records away.

Architecture and the decisions behind it

The whole application is static. That constraint drove most of what follows.

Why no server at all?
A static build on GitHub Pages has no bill, no credentials to leak and nothing to keep patched, so the project can stay live indefinitely without maintenance. Everything a visitor needs is precomputed into GeoJSON, JSON and Parquet at build time; the optional assistant runs in the browser rather than calling anything.
Why precision with a Wilson interval, and no recall figure?
Recall would require knowing every animal actually present, which nobody does. Reporting a number that cannot be computed honestly is worse than reporting none, so the ledger carries precision with an interval and says plainly that recall is unavailable.
Why is raw model output append-only?
Detections from the vision model are never edited in place. Corrections are new rows, so the provenance of any sighting on the map can be traced back to the run that produced it and the confidence it carried at the time.
Why skip the iNaturalist mirror inside GBIF?
GBIF republishes iNaturalist, so ingesting both double-counts the same sighting under two identifiers. The ingest excludes the mirror rather than deduplicating afterwards, because the join key is unreliable and a missed duplicate becomes a false hotspot on a map.

Limitations and scope

This is a map of where people reported animals, which is a map of where people go as much as where animals are: roads, trailheads and popular overlooks are over-represented, and the ledger says so. Several build phases are unfinished and marked as such rather than quietly omitted. The vision-model layer is explicitly supplementary, contributing 31 predicted sightings against a reference dataset of human observations, and is labelled that way on the map.

Data: iNaturalist and GBIF, under their respective licences, with photograph rights checked per record. Sightings are observations reported by the public, not a survey of what is present.
