Research · Explorer

Explore what the studies found

Every number the six research studies produced lives in database tables their notebooks wrote. This page loads those tables directly, so you can see which players landed in each role, search style similarity across 1,350 player-seasons, and sort the watch lists yourself. Nothing here is recomputed or editorialized: it is the studies' own output, made browsable.

Who is in each role cluster

From the role-lens study: pick a lens, then a role, and every player-season assigned to it appears with its fit score and the within-season percentiles that define the lens. Fit is the assignment confidence; a value near zero means the player sits on a boundary between roles, and the study treats those as hybrids rather than clean members. The scoring lens is reported by the study as a descriptive partition, not stable structure.

Style similarity search

From the style-embedding study: pick any player-season and the twelve nearest player-seasons in the 32-dimensional style space appear, with their role labels attached. Distance measures how a player plays, not how well; the study found this space statistically indistinguishable from a PCA equivalent on its held-out test, so read matches as style comparables, not a proprietary edge.

2026-27 trajectory watch list

From the trajectory study: rise and fall probabilities for every qualifying 2025-26 player, written before the season and graded by it. The study's own correction applies here: these probabilities are driven principally by current level, age, prior trend, and the geometry of a bounded percentile scale, and all reported model performance is development evidence. Sort either column and judge the lists yourself.

Clutch trust, every bench player-season

From the bench-clutch study: clutch lift is a player's share of his team's clutch minutes minus his share of ordinary minutes, the coaching decision isolated from playing time. Positive lift marks players a coach promotes when the game is tight. The study's reliability gate applies: trust repeats year over year; clutch performance itself does not, so no column here is a measure of clutch skill.

Team-level results

From the bench-usage study: each team-season's bench profile against its net rating, with starter net rating alongside because the study's central finding is that raw bench numbers mislead without it. From the playoff study: every playoff run with its opponent strength and opponent-adjusted residual, the number that separates a hard bracket from a real underperformance.
