International Scouting Board

A translation pipeline from European leagues to the NBA, built twice: a v2 board of validated per-stat translations, then a v3 hierarchical Bayesian re-evaluation that overturned half of v2's top targets. A stat line moving between leagues is domain shift in disguise, handled with a joint hierarchical Bayesian model, because small transition samples demand pooled strength and a scouting board needs honest uncertainty.

QuestionWhat does a European stat line really mean in NBA terms, and with how much certainty?
Data3 vendor feeds reconciled; 118 INTL-to-NBA transitions; 431 players projected
MethodJoint hierarchical Bayesian translation with a hand-rolled Gibbs sampler; every stat validated against a naive baseline
Result50% lower usage error than naive; 5 of the prior board's top 10 displaced under the stricter re-evaluation
Overview

The project turns scattered, incompatible international data into a defensible answer to a signing decision. Three vendor data feeds with inconsistent conventions are reconciled into one clean history covering NBA and four European league careers, and everything downstream is built on that foundation. Version 2 learned per-stat INTL to NBA translations empirically and validated each against a naive baseline with leave-one-out prediction error. Version 3 rebuilt the pipeline around a joint hierarchical Bayesian translation model with a hand-rolled Gibbs sampler, hardened the ingestion (103 to 118 training transitions, 392 to 431 ranked players), let the data choose the archetypes, and replaced the point ranking with an upside probability computed from posterior draws. Both versions end in a written scouting report; v3 also reports, openly, what it changed about v2's conclusions.

The question

What does a EuroLeague stat line really mean in NBA terms?

Learned from the 118 players who actually made the jump: scoring and usage translate strongly with age priced in, rebounds and assists carry over nearly one for one, and three-point volume fails validation in both versions. The v3 re-evaluation adds the harder lesson: a board is only as good as its test, and under a stricter one, five of v2's top ten fell out of the top ten, with the #1 and #3 targets removed from the board entirely.

Why it matters

International signings commit roster spots and money on the basis of production in leagues with different pace, spacing, and competition levels. Intuition about translation is largely untested, and the analytical question is precise: which parts of a European stat line carry over, by how much, and with what uncertainty? The v2 to v3 arc adds a second question every front office should ask of its own models: when the method improves, which recommendations flip?

The data, and how it was prepared

Three vendor feeds arrive with incompatible schemas and no shared player key: NBA box seasons, international box seasons across four European leagues, and a bio file of names and birth dates. Cleaning is where the project earns its sample. Joins run on unicode-normalized name keys (accents, hyphens, suffixes, casing) because raw-string joins silently split careers in two; duplicate names in the bio file get no birth date rather than a guessed one, and the decision is logged; and missing values stay missing, no zero-fills, a rule reinforced by finding vendor columns that were 100% populated yet 100% zero on the international side, placeholders for stats those leagues never tracked. Features are per-40 rates plus pace-free efficiency and role measures, careers collapse into minutes-weighted league runs ordered INTL before NBA within a season, and eligibility floors (300 international minutes, 100 NBA) apply to aggregated season totals rather than single rows, which is what recovered 39 split-season players. Every exclusion prints as a logged decision, 123 low-minute transitions out by rule, so no player is lost silently.

Method and key decisions

The first decision was where the truth comes from: instead of borrowing league-strength coefficients or scouting folklore, both versions learn translation only from the players who actually made the jump. v2 kept the modeling deliberately simple, ten per-stat regressions, because with 103 transitions the first job was proving any signal existed. Each stat competed in two forms (stat only, stat plus age) on leave-one-out error against the naive assumption that stats carry over unchanged, leave-one-out because 103 examples cannot spare a holdout set, and the payoff was a clean verdict per stat: usage and scoring translate strongly, rebounds and assists carry over, and three-point volume failed its baseline and was down-weighted instead of kept.

v3 asked the question v2 could not answer: how sure are we about any single player? The ingestion hardening above came first because at this sample size data recovery beats model capacity: it grew the training set 15% before a single new equation. Then one joint hierarchical Bayesian model replaced the ten separate fits, chosen not for accuracy (on identical folds it ties per-stat OLS within 1.5% on every stat, and the table below says so) but because pooled slopes and a latent per-player factor produce one coherent posterior, which is what makes per-player uncertainty and an upside probability computable at all. The archetypes moved from an assumed k=3 to a BIC-chosen k=4 Gaussian mixture because the peer group a player is graded against is part of the model, not a styling choice, and the sort key moved from a point composite to P(upside) from 1,000 posterior draws because recruitment is a search for upside, not for the best average, with an honest footnote: the probability sort correlates 0.95 by rank with the mean-composite sort, so its real value is the unit, a probability a front office can act on. What reshuffled the board is the data recovery, the stricter playing-time accounting, and the re-drawn peer groups, not the sampler. Both pipelines stay deterministic and seeded end to end.

How the results were generated

Every number on the board is a model estimate, not an observed stat. Raw vendor feeds pass through hardened ingestion into minutes-weighted career runs; the 118 observed transitions fit the joint model; and each ranked player's posterior produces the translated projections, soft archetype membership, and the upside probability the board prints. The chain, end to end:

3 vendor JSONs→ Hardened ingestion: name keys, ambiguity-safe joins→ Minutes-weighted career runs→ 118 INTL→NBA transitions→ Joint hierarchical Bayes (Gibbs)→ Fold-identical CV: HB vs OLS vs naive→ 431-player pool projection→ Soft archetypes (GMM, BIC k=4)→ P(upside) from 1,000 posterior draws→ Board CSV + report + charts
Validation and reliability

Every translated stat competed against the naive assumption that stats carry over unchanged, on leave-one-out error in v2 and seeded fold-identical cross-validation in v3, and the stat that failed its baseline (three-point volume) was down-weighted rather than kept. The hierarchical model was scored against per-stat OLS and the naive baseline on identical folds, and the sampler's convergence was checked directly rather than assumed. What this establishes is predictive accuracy against stated baselines within this transition sample; it does not correct for who reaches the NBA in the first place, a limitation treated below.

The v2 vs v3 re-evaluation: what a stricter test changed

v3 was validated with seeded 10-fold cross-validation on folds shared by all three competitors: the hierarchical Bayesian model, v2-style per-stat OLS, and the naive carry-over baseline. The comparison came back a tie, and it is reported as one: the Bayesian model matches OLS on point accuracy, with CV error within 1.5% on every stat. Its value is the coherent posterior, which yields per-player uncertainty and the upside-probability ranking. What reshuffled the board is the stricter playing-time legitimacy accounting, the recovered sample, and the re-drawn peer groups: the elegant model moved the error bars, and the boring work moved the answers.

Results
  • The re-evaluation overturned the board. v2's #1 (composite 1.02) and #3 targets fail v3's stricter playing-time legitimacy accounting entirely, and v2's #9 collapses to rank 39 with P(upside) 0.02. A 19-year-old enters the v3 top ten on upside probability that a point ranking missed.
  • Validation held or improved on a larger sample. With 15% more training transitions, usage error fell 50% versus naive and scoring 36%; three-point volume still fails its baseline and stays flagged.
  • Sampler quality is checked, not assumed. Split-half slope drift across 1,000 kept posterior draws is 0.009, and every ingestion exclusion is printed as a logged decision.
  • Known limitations are stated in the README. Including one inherited inconsistency, kept deliberately: 3PM/40 fails the MAE gate yet keeps composite weight 0.16 because weights come from CV correlation. It is documented, with the fix described, rather than silently patched after shipping.
What the results mean

Within this sample of players who actually made the jump, scoring and usage translate strongly once age is priced in, rebounds and assists carry over nearly one for one, and three-point volume does not survive its baseline in either version. The board's rankings are estimates conditional on the model and on survivorship: they order the pool under stated assumptions rather than predict any individual career. The v2-to-v3 comparison is the study's most transferable lesson: half of a top-ten list can change when the evaluation standard tightens, which is an argument for re-testing any board before acting on it.

Limitations and scope

The limitations that matter for interpretation, stated rather than implied:

  • Survivorship, stated in both versions: the transition sample only contains players who reached the NBA, so projections are ranking context, not expectations.
  • Honest clustering: the mixture's fourth component is a residual group of low-event profiles, and the README says so rather than naming it a position.
  • Uncertainty that varies where it should: the raw points band is nearly constant across the pool, so per-player uncertainty is read from the composite's posterior spread instead; making observation noise heteroskedastic in minutes is the named fix.
How it ships

Each version ships as one deterministic, seeded script that regenerates the board CSV, the written scouting report, and the charts from raw data, with versioned branches preserving exactly what each version shipped. A re-run reproduces the outputs byte for byte, which is what makes the v2 vs v3 comparison above auditable.

Technical deep dive

Technical highlights

  • One joint model instead of ten fits: translation slopes partially pool across stats, per-stat age effects share a prior, and a latent per-player factor captures "translates better or worse than their stats say," fit with an all-conjugate Gibbs sampler written from scratch, no MCMC library.
  • Fold-identical three-way CV: hierarchical Bayes, per-stat OLS, and the naive baseline are scored on the same seeded folds, so the comparison is apples to apples by construction.
  • Data-chosen archetypes: BIC selects k=4 over v2's assumed k=3; memberships are soft, so combo profiles are graded against a blended peer group, a membership-entropy column flags straddlers, and a mixture-density pass flags "unicorns" that fit no archetype.
  • Hardened ingestion as a modeling contribution: normalized name keys (accents, hyphens, suffixes) and same-season league ordering grew the training sample 15%; ambiguous duplicate names get no birth date rather than a guessed one.

Key engineering decisions

Why a hierarchical model when OLS ties it on accuracy?
Because the deliverable is a ranking under uncertainty, not a point forecast. Partial pooling produces a coherent joint posterior, which is what makes per-player uncertainty and P(upside) computable at all. The CV table states the point-accuracy tie plainly.
Why a hand-rolled Gibbs sampler?
The model was designed to be all-conjugate, so exact conditional draws are available without an MCMC library, the sampler is fully inspectable, and convergence is checked directly (split-half slope drift 0.009).
Why an upside probability instead of a point rank?
Recruitment lists exist to find upside. P(composite clears the pool's top-decile bar) ranks players by the chance they are worth a roster spot, which is the actual decision, and it is what elevated a 19-year-old the v2 point ranking buried.
Why keep a known inconsistency in the shipped board?
The 3PM weight disagreement between the MAE gate and correlation weighting existed in the shipped v2 board. v3 documents it and describes the fix rather than silently changing a shipped artifact, because reproducibility of what actually shipped matters.

Future improvements

Gate composite weights on the baseline test, which moves weight toward blocks and assists; make observation noise heteroskedastic in minutes; and model the selection process itself, who receives NBA offers, to address survivorship directly.
