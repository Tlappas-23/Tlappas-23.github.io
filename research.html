Research

Six questions, one database, one standard

Six self-contained analyses built on the same five-season NBA database: 97 tables of box score, tracking, hustle, play-type, and lineup-split data, 2021-22 through 2025-26. Each analysis stands alone with its own question, methods, and findings. What they share is the standard: confounds named before results, permutation tests with corrected p-values, resampling units that match the data's dependence structure, feature selection nested inside validation folds, and honest nulls reported as findings.

After all six were finished, I reviewed the suite the way a head of analytics would review an analyst's work, then fixed what the review found instead of arguing with it. Three headline claims came out weaker: a neural network's edge over PCA dissolved on a properly firewalled test, a clustering solution fell below its own pre-registered stability bar, and a fifth of one model's apparent skill turned out to be the geometry of a bounded scale. All three are published in their corrected form, with the correction documented on each page, because a result that survives an honest attempt to kill it is worth more than a bigger number that would not.
